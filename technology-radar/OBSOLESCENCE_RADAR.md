# Obsolescence / Substitution Radar — v0.1

This file tracks technologies whose strategic role may decline when a system architecture changes. It is deliberately conservative: replacement is a hypothesis until deployment evidence exists.

| Current role | Possible substitute / architectural shift | Evidence status | What to verify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Copper data-center interconnect | Optical / co-packaged optics | Strong signal | cost, packaging, reliability |
| Centralized compute for every sensor | Edge / embedded compute | Strong signal | energy and model quality |
| Manual post-process inspection | In-situ metrology | Strong signal | qualification evidence |
| One-off physical prototyping | Simulation + inverse design | Strong signal | physical correlation |
| Freshwater for industrial processes | Reuse + selective separation | Strong signal | lifecycle economics |
| Primary critical-mineral supply | Secondary recovery / recycling | Strong signal | collection economics |
| Static industrial schedules | closed-loop optimization | Emerging | reliability and governance |
| GNSS-only navigation | resilient multi-sensor PNT | Emerging | accuracy, continuity, cost |
| Passive membranes | active/adaptive membrane systems | Emerging | lifetime and scale-up |
| Generic humanoids for all manipulation | task-specific robots + better sensing | Open | real-world economics |

## Anti-hype rule
A technology is not considered obsolete because a substitute exists. Track adoption, installed-base inertia, lifecycle cost, reliability, regulation, and supply-chain effects.
