# Cumulative Model Audit — 2026-08-30

## Audit scope
Review the current repository baseline, prior research rounds, causal graph, simulation radar, foundational-node ranking, evidence policy, and open questions before further forecasting.

## What survives the audit

### High-confidence system patterns
- Measurement + calibration + qualification are increasingly coupled to autonomous manufacturing and advanced technology deployment.
- Interfaces repeatedly govern coupled transport, reaction, stress, heat, charge, optics, aging and failure.
- Simulation has value beyond prediction when connected to UQ/VVUQ, calibration, data assimilation, optimization, inverse design and experiment selection.
- Manufacturing/process capability can dominate the practical value of a scientific discovery.
- Selective separation is a recurring problem across water, minerals, batteries, recycling, chemistry and bioprocessing.
- Degradation and rare events must be treated as state/uncertainty problems for long-lived autonomous systems.

## Claims that must remain provisional
- Any exact 2030/2032/2034 adoption date.
- Universal quantum advantage.
- Universal humanoid superiority over task-specific robotics.
- Fully autonomous scientific discovery at industrial scale.
- Broad economic superiority of active membranes or new separation methods.
- Any single technology as the unavoidable bottleneck for all AI compute.

## Important corrections

1. Do not rank broad labels such as "AI", "quantum", or "robotics" above the enabling layers without causal evidence.
2. Do not treat simulation acceleration as equivalent to discovery acceleration; experiment, qualification, and manufacturing may dominate total cycle time.
3. Do not treat interface improvements as isolated material improvements; evaluate contact mechanics, transport, chemistry, thermal behavior, cycling, manufacturing tolerance and aging together.
4. Treat as-built state, process history and defect distributions as potentially causal variables in physical digital twins.
5. Separate "technology exists" from "technology scales economically".
6. Separate "standard exists" from "interoperability is achieved in heterogeneous deployments".
7. Replace single-score rankings with dimensioned evidence until scores can be empirically calibrated.

## Research quality rule
A candidate moves upward only when at least one of the following becomes concrete:

- measured system-level improvement;
- repeatable experiment under realistic conditions;
- production/manufacturing evidence;
- independent validation;
- standardization accompanied by deployment;
- demonstrated bottleneck removal;
- credible cost/energy advantage.
