# Research Protocol

## Purpose
Build a durable, auditable technology-foresight corpus for 2026–2032, with an extended horizon to 2034 where useful. The project searches for both visible frontier technologies and weak signals hidden in enabling layers, bottlenecks, standards, manufacturing processes, materials, scientific methods, and reactivated older technologies.

## Core principle
Do not rank a technology by publicity. Rank the evidence and the causal position of the technology in a system.

## Research questions
1. What capabilities are likely to become technically or economically important between 2026 and 2032?
2. Which small or obscure technologies can unlock multiple larger systems?
3. Which current bottlenecks can constrain otherwise successful technologies?
4. Which old technologies become strategically important because system conditions changed?
5. Which technologies are converging across domains?
6. Which apparently promising technologies are failing to cross laboratory-to-system or system-to-market thresholds?
7. Which assumptions in mainstream forecasts are weak, false, or incomplete?

## Evidence hierarchy
Prefer, in order when available:
1. Primary measurements, experiments, datasets, standards, and official technical documentation.
2. Government R&D programs and laboratories.
3. Peer-reviewed research and technical reviews.
4. Industrial disclosures with concrete deployment/manufacturing evidence.
5. Patents, standards activity, procurement, and supply-chain evidence.
6. High-quality market analysis.
7. Journalism and commentary as discovery signals, not sole proof of technical claims.

## Search modes
Use multiple independent search paths:
- scientific literature;
- government programs and funding calls;
- standards and roadmaps;
- patents and intellectual-property activity;
- industrial supply-chain evidence;
- manufacturing and equipment capacity;
- datasets and experimental repositories;
- failed demonstrations and negative results;
- expert/community discussion only as lead generation.

## Cross-validation rule
A strong claim should ideally be supported by evidence from at least two independent channels. A single source may establish an observation, but not automatically a forecast.

## Date discipline
The research cutoff is the actual current date of the research session. Do not use future-dated material. Explicitly record publication date and event date when they differ.

## Claim separation
Every important item must distinguish:
- observation: what was directly reported or measured;
- evidence: the source supporting the observation;
- inference: what follows from multiple observations;
- forecast: an uncertain future expectation;
- hypothesis: a proposition requiring further testing.

## Negative evidence
“Not found” is not equivalent to “does not exist”. Record failed searches, missing deployment evidence, contradictory results, and unresolved questions separately.

## Causal-stack test
For important technologies inspect the chain:

phenomenon → material → process → component → interface → measurement → simulation → control → qualification → manufacturing → deployment → failure/recovery.

The project gives additional weight to capabilities that remove a bottleneck or improve multiple stages at once.

## Technology score dimensions
Do not collapse these dimensions prematurely into one number:
- evidence strength;
- maturity;
- bottleneck relevance;
- cross-domain leverage;
- scalability;
- manufacturability;
- cost/energy leverage;
- reliability sensitivity;
- dependency centrality;
- substitutability;
- regulatory/geopolitical exposure;
- time-to-impact;
- uncertainty.

## Required research output
Each research round should leave a reproducible trail containing:
- question;
- search strategy;
- sources;
- observations;
- contradictions;
- promoted signals;
- demoted signals;
- new dependencies;
- open questions;
- next search targets.

## Strategic rule
The project is not intended to predict the future with certainty. It is intended to identify high-leverage uncertainties early enough to investigate them rigorously.
