# Evidence Policy

## Epistemic statuses

- `ESTABLISHED`: directly supported by strong, reproducible evidence.
- `EXPERIMENTALLY_SUPPORTED`: demonstrated under stated experimental conditions.
- `INDUSTRIALLY_DEPLOYED`: evidence of real operational or production deployment.
- `OFFICIAL_PROGRAM`: appears in a government/standards/industrial R&D program.
- `STRONG_SIGNAL`: multiple independent indicators, but future impact remains uncertain.
- `INFERENCE`: reasoned consequence of supported observations.
- `HYPOTHESIS`: explicit proposition requiring testing.
- `SPECULATIVE`: plausible but weakly evidenced.
- `CONTRADICTED`: evidence materially conflicts with the claim.
- `UNKNOWN`: insufficient evidence.
- `OPEN`: important unresolved question.

## Source discipline
A source may prove an observation without proving a forecast. Corporate roadmaps are evidence of intent, not evidence of successful future delivery. Press releases are evidence of claims made by the organization; use independent validation for performance claims whenever possible.

## Freshness
Record:
- publication date;
- date of experiment/deployment;
- date accessed;
- whether the source describes current state, a future target, or historical state.

Exclude material published after the research date from the current evidence base.

## Cross-validation
For high-impact forecasts prefer two or more independent evidence channels, for example:
- government program + peer-reviewed paper;
- industrial deployment + independent measurement;
- standards activity + manufacturing evidence;
- supply-chain constraint + capacity data.

## No hype conversion
Do not convert:
- funding → technical success;
- prototype → production readiness;
- patent → commercial viability;
- benchmark → generalization;
- roadmap → delivery;
- market interest → scientific validity.

## Negative evidence
Record failed or absent evidence precisely. Use `NO_DEPLOYMENT_EVIDENCE`, `CONTRADICTORY_EVIDENCE`, `UNVALIDATED`, or `SEARCH_INCONCLUSIVE` where appropriate rather than broad negative claims.

## Claims must be traceable
Each major claim should reference one or more source IDs in the evidence corpus. Forecasts must also identify the observations and assumptions from which they are derived.
